# AI Policy

Rigs of Rods welcomes contributions made with the assistance of AI tools,
provided they meet the project's standards for quality, maintainability, and
licensing.

This policy applies to contributions to Rigs of Rods repositories, including
code, documentation, issues, pull requests, and other project materials.

## Responsibility

Contributors are responsible for everything they submit, regardless of whether
it was written by hand or generated with AI.

You must understand the changes you submit, be able to explain your
implementation, and verify that the changes work as intended. Submitting code
that you cannot reasonably understand or explain is not acceptable.

AI tools may assist with development, debugging, research, and documentation,
but they do not replace the contributor's responsibility to review and validate
their output.

## Disclosure

You must disclose the use of AI tools that materially contributed to the
development of your pull request.

Your disclosure should identify the tool or tools used and briefly explain
how they contributed to the changes. Where practical, identify which parts
of the contribution where generated or substantially modified by AI.

Routine autocomplete, spelling correction, and grammar assistance do not
require disclosure.

Undisclosed material AI assistance may be grounds for closing a pull request.

## Code Quality

All contributions must meet the same quality standards, regardless of how they
were produced.

We will reject contributions that are incorrect, unnecessarily complicated,
poorly tested, inconsistent with the existing codebase, or otherwise unsuitable
for the project.

We will not accept AI slop: code or other material generated with little
meaningful review, verification, or consideration of whether it belongs in the
project.

Do not submit speculative changes, unnecessary refactoring,
bulk-generated fixes, or cosmetic changes simply because an AI tool suggested
them. Contributions should address a concrete problem or an improvement that
has been discussed and agreed upon where appropriate.

Maintainers are not obligated to spend time correcting, debugging, or
validating a contributor's generated output.

## AI Agents

AI coding agents may be used as development tools under meaningful human
supervision.

Autonomous contributions are NOT accepted! An AI agent must not independently
identify work, generate a change, and submit an issue or pull request.

Before submission, a human contributor must review the complete change, verify
its correctness, ensure it meets this policy, and take responsibility for it.

Automated agents must not submit batches of speculative changes or open
unsolicited pull requests across the codebase. For substantial or broad changes,
seek maintainer agreement before implementation.

Pull requests or issues that appear to have been submitted autonomously WILL be
closed without review.

## Communication

Issues, pull requests, and discussions should reflect the contributor's own
understanding and intentions.

AI tools may be used to assist with translation, spelling, and grammar, but
contributors must be able to explain their proposals and respond to questions
themselves. DO NOT use generated explanations to misrepresent your
understanding of a change.

Contributions are expected to engage constructively with maintainers and other
community members throughout the review process.

## Maintainer Discretion

Maintainers may reject or close any contribution that does not meet this policy
or the project's contribution requirements. They are not obligated to provide a
detailed review or remediation plan for low-quality, irrelevant, or
insufficiently review submissions.

These requirements apply equally to all contributors, regardless of the tools
they use.
